# How to study

Start from requirements, not product names. Build a specification table. Check electrical, mechanical and software compatibility. Record why a part was selected and why alternatives were rejected.

# Labs

1) Create a component requirement. 2) Compare candidates. 3) Read datasheets. 4) Check electrical ratings. 5) Check interface/mechanical compatibility. 6) Record the final engineering decision.

# Gandaberunda connection

Procurement must follow the V1 architecture and prevent incompatible, unnecessary or premature purchases.

# Must know

Requirements vs specifications; datasheets; ratings; interfaces; compatibility; alternatives; cost; decision records.

# Pass test

Both developers can justify a component against requirements and identify why a seemingly suitable component should be rejected.
